# UnboltKit: decision for the overall prize

Owner's decision, November 23, 2026 · Starting commit: `5137dad`. This is the governing brief for the main entry. Older film documents contain unverified superlatives and are not the source of truth for claims.

**Enter Skeleton Crew. Open with one service question: “Can I take the red ring off without removing the shaft?”** Compare two versions of one small assembly, keeping the shaft, target, camera and requested upward movement fixed. In A, the upper stop is integral to the shaft and traps the ring. In B, the cap is a separate rigid body: remove the cap, retry the same pull, and the ring exits while the shaft stays in place. Guide Forge turns that result into a short guide; Blitz lets someone else try it. The move inspector is the common mechanism. The goal is for a designer reviewing a revision to distinguish a trapped service part from one that can be reached.

## How the council evaluated it

Three reviewers worked independently from the source checkout and public evidence: one took the judge's perspective, one tried to break the geometry, and one represented the product user and the person watching the demo. They made separate recommendations before the judge cross-examined the combined concept. They are critiques of the evidence, not three independent estimates of the odds.

- **Judging seat:** lead with a useful deliverable for a guide author, preserve a genuinely different game and a short developer starter. Would veto a demonstration that silently picks another direction or removes the shaft.
- **Engineering seat:** prefer the built cap pair over a hand-authored animation. A and B produce different real outcomes across both application import/worker paths and the Node auditor. Found and reproduced a false clear on a three-box assembly; solving this comes before a generic green clearance verdict.
- **Product seat:** lead with the consequence of a design revision for one person replacing one part. Strongest impact evidence would be a real maker or technician comparing the model with a physical task. A mounted microSD case is a plausible service problem but can be a *grip* or tool-access problem; a card-only translation cannot validate finger access.
- **Decision:** the repair-revision story carries the pitch, the fixed-direction inspector is the feature, the guide is the deliverable, the game is the second use, and the separate external model tests reuse. No new camera gesture, extra campaign level or full CAD editor is needed. CI is supporting evidence unless a live integration works; it gets no time at the expense of the main entry.

## Evidence and the non-negotiable technical gate

- Try [revision A: integral cap](probes/service-ring/integral-cap.glb) and [revision B: removable cap](probes/service-ring/removable-cap.glb). These are controlled original fixtures, not a customer's CAD. Both actual upload loaders and solver pipelines were exercised in Node; both agree on the plans. A: two parts, zero moves, ring/spindle cycle. B: three parts, cap then ring then spindle for the **full** teardown; stop after step two to service the ring. Cap moves 2 mm along its exit. The game accepts cap then ring. CLI independently re-run on the saved files. B was checked at 201 poses per move on these meshes; this is a finite test.
- Unchanged [external CC0 mould](https://www.innerscene.com/tools/library/3d-parts/two-plate-mould-open-0cad1380): 23-step plan in both pipelines; headless export of 23 steps. The upper plate's upward move records hits on a locating ring before removal and none after. The [technical benchmark](grand-prize-benchmark.md) includes the file digest. This is generalization evidence, not a certified repair procedure or user study. On the larger external Orbiter, only 53 of 173 solver parts were removed; the remaining cycle is a search result, not proof the machine is unserviceable.
- **Known false clear:** [uploadable crossed bars](probes/crossed-bars.glb) · [reproduction source and commands](probes/crossed-bars.ts). The current solver chooses a +X translation and the independent ray checker reports zero, but the compiled paths intersect at 75% of the first step and have positive-volume overlap at about five-sixths. This was reproduced on unchanged source, independently of the author of the fixture. It is a blocker for an unqualified “Verified / clear to move” label.
- Time-box a shared **finite, straight-translation veto** or explicit “unverified” state. Preserve previous solver and oracle independence; don't make an independent check call the same narrow-phase implementation and then call it corroboration. Test the failing bars, a nearby real-clear control, rotated/renamed/reordered inputs, starting contact vs penetration and resource limits. No shape/name special case, blanket failure or passing on timeout. Dense frame sampling is not continuous verification. Until evidence supports clearance for the selected movement, say “no sampled hits” and mark it unverified. Outside this scope: rotations in the collision claim, tools, hand clearance, gravity, threaded fits, minimum removals, and universal physical safety.
- **Release guard:** a known bad path must be rejected or marked unverified by default anywhere it would otherwise get a green verdict (studio, game for moves presented as checked, CLI/exports/README as applicable). A near-miss control must behave differently. Pin the shaft; stop the targeted guide as soon as the ring clears. Both apps must use the same bytes and named direction, regardless of what the existing general game click chooses.
